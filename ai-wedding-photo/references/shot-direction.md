# Shot Direction

Build the set as a visual story, not a collection of similar portraits. Generate each frame separately.

## Required Directing Table

Before writing prompts, create a table with these columns:

| Frame | Story beat | Shot size | Composition | Interaction/action | Expressions/gaze | Camera/foreground | Location/prop |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Every row must differ from adjacent rows in at least four columns.

## Diversity Quotas

For a 7-9 image set, include:

- At least 2 environmental wide or full-body frames
- At least 2 medium frames showing hands and interaction
- At least 2 close-ups, including one intimate facial close-up
- At least 1 detail or unusual viewpoint: rings, veil, hands, reflection, doorway, overhead, low angle, or foreground framing
- At least 3 moving or task-based actions
- At least 2 quiet or still emotional moments
- At least 1 solo portrait of either partner when the style reference supports it

Across the set:

- Use at least 5 distinct actions.
- Use at least 4 expression/gaze patterns: laughing, mutual gaze, eyes closed, looking away, surprised, peaceful, camera-aware.
- Use at least 3 composition patterns: centered, off-center, framed by architecture/foliage, foreground occlusion, silhouette, overhead.
- Use at least 3 camera heights or viewpoints.
- Limit direct-to-camera gaze to 2 frames.
- Limit formal side-by-side standing poses to 2 frames.
- Do not repeat the same background, body arrangement, and shot size together.

## Action Vocabulary

Prefer observable actions:

- Walk quickly while holding hands; one partner looks back and laughs
- Adjust a veil, collar, boutonniere, hair strand, or cuff
- Lean together to read, cook, pour tea, pack, decorate, or inspect a photograph
- Sit shoulder-to-shoulder, then turn toward each other
- Whisper into an ear and capture the other person's reaction
- Dance, spin, run, cycle, climb stairs, open a door, or step into sunlight
- Rest forehead-to-forehead with eyes closed
- Recline on grass or a sofa and talk quietly
- Embrace from behind while the front partner turns and smiles
- Hold hands in an extreme close-up with rings and fabric texture

Do not use generic phrases like "romantic pose" without a concrete action.

## Expression Direction

Direct reactions, not only face labels:

- One partner says something funny; the other breaks into an unguarded laugh
- They make eye contact after walking and both soften into a small smile
- One partner looks at the other; the other remains unaware and looks toward the window
- Both close their eyes during a forehead touch
- A playful surprise, restrained grin, peaceful pause, or breathless post-run laugh

Avoid giving every frame the same neutral smile.

## Style-Specific Shot Recipes

Use these as a starting sequence and adapt details to the user's references.

### Japanese Film Natural

Doorway greeting wide; walking through an old lane; reading together at a desk; game or kitchen activity; hair adjustment by a window; laughing peek through a door; hands and everyday-object detail; quiet seated portrait; running hand-in-hand at sunset.

### Korean Minimal Airy

Architectural full-body wide with negative space; close shoulder lean; walking across window light with dress movement; seated mutual gaze with interlocked hands; intimate forehead or cheek close-up; one solo bridal portrait; veil or ring detail; playful turn or small dance; off-center silhouette behind sheer curtains.

Do not let the entire set become standing portraits against the same white wall. Use floor seating, window edge, curtain layers, steps, blocks, and moving fabric to create depth.

### Seaside Vacation Documentary

Running at the waterline; cottage porch conversation; windblown facial close-up; cycling side-by-side; rocky-shore landscape wide; spontaneous laugh after splashing; convertible interior; picnic preparation; sunset back-view walk.

### French Vintage Garden

Manor establishing wide; kiss or whisper under dappled light; tea-table interaction; doorway entrance; bouquet or lace detail; solo portrait in patterned shadow; garden stair movement; dance framed through a window; seated laughter among flowers.

### Sayram Lake Monet

Lake-and-mountain establishing wide; veil lifted by wind; walking through meadow overhead; quiet embrace medium; painterly solo bride; seated conversation among flowers; hands against textured fabric; back-view lakeshore walk; lying in grass with soft mutual gaze.

### School Uniform to Marriage

Studying together; graduation portrait; laptop or moving-box domestic transition; proposal reaction; walking through a remembered lane; present-day wedding portrait echoing an earlier pose; hand/ring timeline detail; ceremony movement; final celebratory exit.

### Road Trip Wedding

Convertible close interaction; gas-station stop; roadside shop activity; map planning on hood; coastline or highway aerial wide; back-seat quiet moment; motel or cabin doorway; night porch conversation; sunset road silhouette.

### Pet Family Wedding

Family sofa portrait; dog-walking wide; cat close-up with bride; cooking while pet watches; floor-level play; pet-and-partner individual portrait; paws/rings detail; family cuddle; sleepy bedside ending.

### Golden Cinematic Garden

Lakeside silhouette beneath a large tree; backlit near-kiss close-up; running through flowers; solo bridal portrait with luminous veil; picnic laughter; overhead reclining frame; hands and flowers detail; terrace architecture wide; quiet embrace as sunlight passes through foliage.

## Final Diversity Check

Before delivering or generating, answer internally:

1. Are any three frames essentially the same pose with different crops?
2. Are more than two frames set against the same flat background?
3. Do at least three frames show real movement or an activity?
4. Are wide, medium, close, and detail views all represented?
5. Are expressions and gaze directions visibly different?
6. Does each frame have a concrete action and emotional beat?

If any answer fails, rewrite the shot plan first.
